## The case for adding none of them

**1. There is no additive slot. Every proposal is an eviction.**

`/Users/rasul/senior-fde-dashboard/data/curriculum.json` holds 119 topics, 2,236 subtopics, and **zero** subtopics without a resource. The README states the contract: "12–20 parts each with exactly what to learn and **one public resource**." So `slot: "add-as-visual-primer"` and `slot: "subtopic-resource"` are not additions — they are deletions with a replacement. Name what dies. Row 95 subtopic "Momentum and Nesterov acceleration" currently points at Distill's *Why Momentum Really Works*; row 114 "Receptive field arithmetic" at Distill's *Computing Receptive Fields*; row 107 "t-SNE: the KL objective, perplexity" at Distill's *How to Use t-SNE Effectively*; row 106 "Bayesian optimisation: surrogate plus acquisition" at Distill's *Exploring Bayesian Optimization*. The list's own top-ranked recommendation is to install, at four rows, a thing that is already installed at those exact four rows.

**2. Two of the strongest "keeps" are already in the plan, at the mapped rows.** Distill appears 5× (rows 95, 106, 107, 114, 115). Bycroft's LLM Visualization appears at row 116, subtopic "Attention-head heatmaps: producing them and reading them honestly." Both came back `verdict: keep`, `mappingRefuted: true`, `strength: exact`. The mapping pass never diffed against the incumbent set. That is not a small slip — it means the "exact match" score measures *topical overlap*, not marginal value, and every remaining "exact" ranking inherits that bias.

**3. The mapper marked itself unreliable and was ignored.** 14 of 28 entries carry `claimAccurate: false`; 26 of 28 carry `mappingRefuted: true`. The corrections are not cosmetic. ML Visualized has zero diffusion, transformer, attention, convolution or dropout content (0 search-index hits) despite being sold on diffusion models. Gradient Descent Visualizer has zero SGD, momentum or Adam despite being sold on comparing them. Distill has zero AutoML. Embedding Projector has zero GloVe. ConvNet Playground *inverts* its mechanism — Lucid feature visualization answers "what synthetic input maximally excites this channel," not "which neurons fired for this pattern." REINFORCEjs has no policy gradients. The DBSCAN page has no k-means comparison. Half the source claims were wrong, and the recommendation was still `keep` for 24 of 28.

**4. Chronology destroys the "visual primer" premise.** A primer works before the formalism. Here it would land after. Rows 111–114 are month 22; rows 115–119 are month 23. By month 22 the learner has completed row 94 linear algebra (month 2), row 95 calculus with micrograd and the Distill momentum article (month 4), row 96 probability (month 5), row 99 information theory including log-sum-exp (month 11), row 100 numerical computing (month 11). TF Playground's payload — more neurons bend the boundary; hand-picked features solve the spiral — is a month-2 intuition delivered to a person whose row 111 depth target reads "hand-derive and hand-code the backward pass so a finite-difference gradient check agrees to ~1e-7" and whose row 112 target is "implement SGD-with-momentum, Adam and warmup+cosine from scratch and match torch.optim to within 1e-6." Seeing Theory is the same error one track over: an AP-statistics site mapped to row 96 (month 5, incumbent: Blitzstein Stat 110, scipy.stats.bootstrap, conjugate-prior tables) whose depth target is "derive a posterior over a rate by hand and reproduce it in NumPy." Seeing Theory's own audit names its failure mode as "spend an hour, feel fluent in Bayesian inference, and still be unable to write a conjugate update or a bootstrap loop." That failure mode *is* the row-96 depth target, stated as a known defect of the resource.

**5. Five independent audits reached the same verdict about the category, unprompted.** TF Playground: "you finish with feel and no ability to compute." Setosa: "You will feel why PC1 is PC1; you will not be able to compute it afterwards." TensorSpace: "you leave with a picture, not an implementation." Embedding Projector: "you will leave with strong geometric intuition but unable to implement t-SNE or UMAP." Diffusion Explainer: "you finish unable to map its vocabulary onto diffusers code." This plan is built specifically against that failure. Every row carries a `depth` target phrased as a production act, `failureModes` phrased as incidents, `outcomes` phrased closed-book, and `data/recall-bank.json` grades against them. A resource whose payload is recognition rather than recall is a resource the assessment layer cannot score.

**6. Toy scale actively miseducates for *this* job.** TF Playground's transferable lesson — tick `sin(x1)` and `x1²` and the spiral falls out — is the inverse of representation learning; its own audit says the lesson "can actively mislead if taken as general advice." GAN Lab offers log loss and LSGAN only: no WGAN-GP, no spectral norm, no FID, on an architecture the audit itself says diffusion displaced — and it maps to row 99, where the incumbent for the same JS-divergence point is Goodfellow et al. 2014, which is the artifact you cite to a customer. Worst: R2D3 part 2 presents the U-curve as "foundational to modeling well," while row 101 subtopic 19 is literally "Double descent: the caveat to the U-curve you must not overstate" (Belkin et al.). Integrating it imports a claim the plan inoculates against eighteen subtopics later.

**7. The rot is measured, not hypothetical, and it will turn the repo's own CI red.** Verified today (2026-09-07): `https://www.benfrederickson.com/numerical-optimization/` fails TLS with `ssl_verify_result=10` (expired certificate); `http://www.r2d3.us/visual-intro-to-machine-learning-part-2/` returns HTTP 522; `https://gpt2.apps.allenai.org/` — the one interactive demo The Illustrated GPT-2 links to — refuses connection. `/Users/rasul/senior-fde-dashboard/scripts/verify_curriculum_urls.py` classifies 522 as `dead` and a TLS failure as `error`. Beyond those: AttentionViz is structurally dead (HTTPS frontend calling a plain-HTTP bare-IP backend, with HSTS `max-age=31556952` foreclosing the workaround permanently); ML Visualiser fabricates output — its CNN reads the ground-truth label and assigns it 70–90% confidence, and its "gradient descent" animation interpolates frames then `.sort()`s them by descending error; REINFORCEjs injects MathJax over `http://` onto an `https` page so every equation renders as raw LaTeX; CNN Explainer still requests the sunset `polyfill.io`; Setosa runs AngularJS 1.x (EOL Dec 2021) with a webcam button calling `URL.createObjectURL(MediaStream)`, removed in Chrome 71. The existing resource set is 135 arXiv, 101 scikit-learn, 81 PyTorch docs, 44 d2l.ai, 31 Hugging Face — owners with institutional maintenance and a decade horizon. Interactive third-party demos currently appear 4 times in 2,236 subtopics (Secret Lives of Data and RaftScope at row 45, VisuAlgo at row 93, Bycroft at row 116), plus 5 Distill articles: **0.4%**, and every one is a case where the subject is a state machine you genuinely cannot hold in your head. That sparsity is the editorial policy. Twenty additions would triple the class and change what the plan is.

**8. There is no slack to spend.** 1,614 hours, 119 rows, 23 months — 15.9 h/week sustained, minimum month 14.8, maximum 19.2, no light month anywhere. 59,676 subtopic-minutes (994.6 h) are itemised to the minute. The 28 sites' own claimed read-times total 738 minutes (12.3 h), and that is the optimistic number: ML Visualiser's XOR demo needs 7,668–10,413 epochs at a hard ceiling of 5 epochs/sec (26–35 minutes staring at a number that does not move, ~10 hours if backgrounded); Transformer Explainer cold-downloads 626 MB in 63 chunks; TensorSpace's VGG16 is 527 MB; AttentionViz ships 68 MB per model. And the deep-learning block collides with the loop: month 22 is 69 h (rows 110–114), month 23 is 73.5 h (rows 115–119), while track K mocks run months 19–21 and applications go out month 19 (row 92). Integration cost is also not zero — edit `data/curriculum/NN.json`, re-run `scripts/build-curriculum.py`, re-run the URL verifier, and re-derive recall-bank entries for every changed subtopic.

## What survives

Criteria: it must show *live state* a paper or doc page cannot; it must land on a subtopic whose incumbent is weaker or off-target (upgrade, not eviction); it must pass the repo's URL checker and have a maintainer; and its payload must be gradeable against that row's depth target.

**1. BertViz → row 115, subtopic "Attention heat maps as a diagnostic instrument" (20 min). Take it.** This is the one clean upgrade in the list. The incumbent is Olah & Carter, *Attention and Augmented Recurrent Neural Networks* (Distill, 2016) — and the Distill audit itself flags exactly this article as pre-transformer, teaching attention through Neural Turing Machines and adaptive computation time, and says it "will not help with modern LLM attention." The subtopic says *instrument*: something you run. BertViz is pip-installable, Apache-2.0, and works on any HF model with `output_attentions=True`. It is also the only item on the list that produces an FDE-shaped artifact — a notebook cell you run against a customer's own model in front of them. The stale README (`attn_implementation="eager"` now required under transformers v5) is a feature here: discovering that SDPA does not return attentions is precisely the material in row 116's final subtopic. Record the limits in the entry: neuron view is BERT/GPT-2/RoBERTa only via a vendored 2019 fork, and issue #131 means no attention over autoregressively generated tokens.

**2. Transformer Explainer → row 50, replacing the broken `Do` link. Take it, after fixing the link on its own merits.** Row 50's Do is labelled "Karpathy: Let's build GPT" and points at `https://www.youtube.com/@AndrejKarpathy` — a channel root, not a lecture. That is a defect regardless of this list, and the minimal fix is `watch?v=kCc8FmEb1nY`. But that URL is already row 116's Watch, so the minimal fix creates a duplicate. Transformer Explainer is the better occupant *specifically at row 50*: month 12, AI Engineering track, ten months before the from-scratch transformer — the one place in the entire plan where a primer is chronologically ahead of the formalism instead of behind it. Row 50's depth target is client-facing ("read a model card critically… translating all of this into client language"), and two of its subtopics — "Self-attention: Q, K, V and the causal mask, at the level where you can draw it" (40 min) and "Decoding and sampling: temperature, top_p, top_k" (35 min) — are exactly a live GPT-2 with temperature, top-k and top-p controls. Record: 626 MB cold load, 12-word prompt cap, desktop-only input, and GPT-2-era architecture — no RoPE, GQA or RMSNorm, which row 116 subtopics 9 and 10 supply as the correction.

**3. Netron — conditional, and not where the mapping put it.** `onnx`, `safetensors` and `gguf` each appear **zero** times across all 2,236 subtopics. Row 12 ("Serving models on Kubernetes and GPUs") and row 62 ("Serving at scale") are entirely vLLM- and HF-Hub-shaped and quietly assume the weights arrive as a Hugging Face repo. An FDE gets handed an artifact. That is a real gap and Netron is the right tool for it — but filling it means *adding a subtopic* in track B or H (month 3 or 15), not swapping a resource in track O at month 22, which is what the mapping proposed (rows 113/114/116/117). If the rule is "no new subtopics," Netron drops with the rest. If it goes in, record the trap: a plain `.pth` state_dict renders as a flat weight inventory, not a graph — you need TorchScript, `torch.export` or ONNX first.

**Everything else: no.** Including the near-misses, which deserve their reasons on the record. CNN Explainer is the most tempting cut — but its hyperparameter widget *computes output dimensions for you*, and row 114's depth target is "hand-compute the output shape, parameter count and receptive field of any conv stack." It automates the exact skill the row grades. Naftali Harris's DBSCAN page has no k-distance guidance, no HDBSCAN, and a non-reference implementation that lets cluster B steal cluster A's border points, while row 107's incumbents are scikit-learn's own DBSCAN demo and k-distance material that match production semantics. Gradient Descent Visualizer is a dead certificate pointed at Rosenbrock and Himmelblau, which row 95's final subtopic already covers with `scipy.optimize.rosen` — runnable code instead of a click-through interstitial. And the TF Playground spiral entry is, by its own audit, the same URL as the TF Playground entry with a different fragment; carrying both would inflate the count by one and teach nothing twice.
