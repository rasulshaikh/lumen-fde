# Folding the 28-site visual-ML list into the Senior FDE plan - decision

## 1. VERDICT

Fold in **four items, as four evictions, plus one unrelated link repair** - and reject the other twenty-two artifacts outright. The list's premise ("visual understanding sticks 10x longer than reading") is unsupported, and the protocol it prescribes ("pick one concept, spend 30 minutes interacting") is the passive-viewing condition that Hundhausen's algorithm-visualization meta-study found statistically indistinguishable from no visualization; but the premise being wrong does not make the artifacts worthless, and "add nothing" is the wrong answer because the challenge pass left four specific per-match claims standing against incumbents that are demonstrably weaker *for the exact subtopic they occupy*. The four are: **bbycroft's LLM Visualization relocated within row 116 from subtopic 17 to subtopic 0** (it currently sits on a slot it provably cannot serve - 3-token `{A,B,C}` vocabulary, no head selector, so you cannot *produce* a heatmap with it); **BertViz into the row 116 subtopic 17 that bbycroft vacates**; **Transformer Explainer into row 50 subtopic 3**, displacing arXiv:1706.03762, which is not lost because the same paper is already the resource at row 115 subtopic 19 and row 116 subtopic 2 - the two rows where the learner actually derives it; and **Naftali Harris's DBSCAN visualization into row 107 subtopic 8**, the single per-match claim in all 26 challenge verdicts that the auditor recorded as surviving. Total cost: zero minutes, zero hours, zero new subtopics, four resource URLs changed and one broken `Do` URL fixed. Everything else on the list either duplicates an incumbent, contradicts a subtopic the plan wrote specifically to correct it, teaches the front half of a subtopic name and none of the discriminating half, or is dead.

GAN Lab loads with `<paper-checkbox id="toggle-pretrained" checked>` so pressing play shows a converged equilibrium a learner reports as "I watched a GAN train"; its KL/JS charts are an external histogram diagnostic in bits over sample grids, not the model's loss, while the ln 2 anchor is in nats in a different panel and nothing connects them. **Workbook `Plan` - one row, two cells.** `data/workbook.json` → `Plan`, row index 50 (header is index 0, so plan rows are 1-indexed by row number). Columns 10 (`Do (lab / game / build)`) and 11 (`Do URL`). No other Plan cell changes, on any row.

**Does `Hours` move? No - and it could not.** `Hours` (column 13) is not derived from subtopic minutes: across rows 94-119 subtopic minutes total 12,234 against 20,100 declared minutes, a uniform ~5 h/row `proofOfWork` reserve. All four swaps are minute-for-minute (25, 30, 40, 25 unchanged), so even the subtopic total is invariant. The plan stays at 1,614 h, 119 rows, 23 months.

**Does `renumber-months.py` re-run? No.** It keys exclusively off the `Hours` column (`months = round(before_hours / (16.0 x 52/12))`) and asserts `diff in ([], [MONTH_COL])` - "a column other than Month changed." Hours is unchanged, so it would relabel nothing. Running it is not merely unnecessary, it is contraindicated - see the landmine.

**The landmine, and it must be handled before `build-curriculum.py` is run at all.** `build-curriculum.py` assigns `topics[str(i)] = d` - the whole per-topic file, including its own `month` and `hours` keys. Those keys are stale in **119 of 119** files. Verified examples: `49.json` says `month: 7, hours: 10` against the Plan's `12.0 / 15.5`; `115.json` says `13 / 12` against `23.0 / 17.0`; `116.json` says `13 / 10` against `23.0 / 16.0`; `106.json` says `12.0 / 7` against `21.0 / 12.5`; `00.json` says `1 / 8` against `1.0 / 14.0`. `renumber-months.py --write` previously synced `curriculum.json`'s month values directly (it patches `cur["topics"][str(i)]["month"]` in place) but never touched the per-topic files. The builder *detects* the drift - it appends to `problems` - but **only exits non-zero under `--strict`**, which is not the default. So `python3 scripts/build-curriculum.py` today silently reverts month and hours in `curriculum.json` for every topic in the plan, which would make the calendar lie and, per the builder's own comment, is "the same class of stale number this script exists to remove."

**Order of operations:**

1. Reconcile `month` and `hours` in all 119 `data/curriculum/NN.json` files against `data/workbook.json` `Plan` columns 1 and 13 - or, better, patch `build-curriculum.py` to take `month`/`hours` from `plan_rows[i]` rather than from the file, removing the drift class permanently. Do this **first and separately**; it is not part of this decision.
2. Apply the four `resource` edits and the four `learn` trap paragraphs.
3. Apply the row-50 `Do` / `Do URL` repair in `data/workbook.json`.
4. `python3 scripts/build-curriculum.py --strict` - merges to `data/curriculum.json` and regenerates `data/recall-bank.json`. `--strict` so any surviving drift fails loudly.
5. `python3 scripts/verify_curriculum_urls.py` - it reads `data/curriculum/*.json`, writes `.tmp/url-check.tsv`, and classifies 522 as `dead` and TLS failure as `error`. All four new URLs return 200 today; this is the regression guard, and also the reason none of the rotted candidates (Gradient Descent Visualizer's cert expired 2026-08-28; `www.r2d3.us` returning Cloudflare 522; `gpt2.apps.allenai.org` refusing connection) could be adopted even if their content held.
6. Do **not** run `renumber-months.py`.

**Recall bank: unchanged, and verify it.** `build-curriculum.py` derives `recall-bank.json` from `interviewQuestions` (835 `recall`) and `failureModes` (875 `drill`), plus `meta[i].outcomes[:3]` as the reveal text. None of the four edits touches any of those three fields, so the bank must come back at 1,710 prompts, 835/875, and `eligible()` must still yield **357** cards, all `recall`. If either number moves, something other than a resource URL changed.

## 7. WHAT I WOULD NOT DO

**I would not fix the scheduler as a precondition.** Pedagogy-pass1's closing line is "Fix the scheduler first. Then the sites are worth adding." The finding is real - 875 drills unreachable, `card.kind === "drill"` dead in practice, `PER_TOPIC = 3` plus array ordering guaranteeing it - but it is a separate change with a separate risk profile, and it is not a precondition for four minute-neutral URL swaps that touch no prompt. Reserving one of the three per-topic slots for the first `drill` is a good idea and should be its own decision. Coupling it here blocks a cheap correction behind an expensive one.

**I would not adopt CNN Explainer, however tempting.** Its output-dimension widget genuinely is a marking scheme. But there is no subtopic it can occupy without net loss: row 114 subtopic 1 would lose dilation, groups and the 1x1 convolution to gain padding and stride; subtopic 0 would lose the cross-correlation distinction that d2l's page is named for. And row 114's depth bar is "hand-compute the output shape, parameter count and receptive field of any conv stack" - a slider that recomputes it for you is the wrong instrument at the wrong row.

**I would not install Netron anywhere.** The gap it points at is the most valuable finding in the whole exercise and should be logged as a missing subtopic in track B or H. But installing the tool without an export step is installing a graph viewer for artifacts the plan never produces: a plain `.pth` and a safetensors file both render as a flat weight inventory that `sd.keys()` prints better.

**I would not resurrect ML Visualiser as a "spot the fraud" exercise.** Pedagogy-pass1 floats it as a leakage anti-example for row 104. The answer is "read the source," which generalises to nothing; there is no repo, author or license, so nothing is auditable; and row 104 already teaches metric-flatters-a-broken-model with an exercise that produces a committed artifact. Its XOR demo also cannot finish in a study session - 7,668-10,413 epochs at a hard ceiling of 5 epochs/sec, ~26-35 minutes watching "Current Error: 0.49" not move, and roughly ten hours if the tab is backgrounded under `setTimeout` throttling.

**I would not put AttentionViz on a re-check list.** An HTTPS frontend calling `http://18.219.70.154:8500` with the site's own HSTS header (`max-age=31556952`) foreclosing the http workaround permanently, against an unmanaged EC2 box with no domain, does not get better by 2028. Do not schedule it and do not schedule a check on it.

**I would not carry "TF Playground spiral preset" as a distinct entry.** It is the same origin as TensorFlow Playground differing by a URL fragment. Its one genuinely valuable property - the stock 4-2 net plateauing at test loss ≈ 0.38 through 4,590 epochs, a clean reproducible under-capacity signature - belongs in row 112's `optimiser-parity` proof-of-work as a five-minute warm-up recipe written into `learn` prose, not as a resource URL. Same for TF Playground itself: row 112's depth bar enumerates six broken configs (LR too high, bad init, dead units, exploding/vanishing gradients, BatchNorm wrong mode at eval, fp16 overflow) and the tool can manufacture exactly one.

**I would not touch row 101 subtopic 18 with R2D3 part 2.** That subtopic - "Double descent: the caveat to the U-curve you must not overstate," 10 min, Belkin et al. - exists specifically to correct the claim R2D3 part 2 makes.

**I would not treat "refuted = true" as "nothing survived," and I would not treat "exact match" as "adopt."** The verification schema put one boolean over a whole bundle of matches, so a single overstated match sank every honest one beside it; the real per-match result is only in the reasoning text. The converse trap is worse: the list's top-ranked "exact matches" include recommendations to install Distill at rows 95, 106, 107, 114 and 115 and bycroft at row 116 - resources already installed at those exact rows. The mapper measured topical overlap and reported it as marginal value. Every ranking in the list inherits that bias, which is why the only defensible unit of decision is a named subtopic, a named incumbent, and a stated reason the incumbent is worse **for that subtopic**.
