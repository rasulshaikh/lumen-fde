# Which JD-derived gap claims survived adversarial challenge - decision

## 1. VERDICT

**Zero of eighteen survived.** Every gap was challenged on three independent lenses - find-the-coverage, jd-evidence, worth-closing - and all eighteen were refuted on at least one; thirteen were refuted on two or more, and five (`customer-enablement-training`, `public-sector-acquisition`, `agent-frameworks-named`, `synthetic-data-generation`, `distillation-slm`) were refuted on all three. The honest consequence is that **no subtopic is added, no subtopic is evicted, no hour moves, and `data/curriculum/NN.json` is not touched.** The failure was not random: `worth-closing` refuted 18/18, `find-the-coverage` refuted 13/18, `jd-evidence` refuted 9/18, and the three dominant death causes were (a) the gap was measured by keyword against subtopic *names* while the concept was taught in `learn` bodies and `resource` labels under different words, (b) the JD frequency was inflated by location-and-seniority clones of a single requisition or drawn from a "Bonus Points" block, and (c) the plan-side premise was simply **factually false** - I independently confirmed six gap claims that assert a zero which is not a zero. The one action this exercise justifies is a repair to `/Users/rasul/senior-fde-dashboard/data/market-skill-map.json`, the shipped gap registry, which currently tells the dashboard "No plan row covers this" about six things the plan demonstrably covers.

## 2. THE SURVIVORS

**None.** Listing a survivor here would require overriding a lens verdict, and no verdict is weak enough to override. What follows is the single closest call, recorded so the decision is auditable, plus the residual work that is real but is not a gap.

### Closest call - `emea-ai-regulatory` (killed by one lens only)

The only gap of the eighteen killed by exactly one lens. Coverage confirmed the acronym set is genuinely absent. jd-evidence confirmed the strongest single piece of evidence in the entire corpus: Anthropic "Applied AI Security Architect," London (`job-boards.greenhouse.io/anthropic/jobs/5227641008`), £190,000-£230,000, carrying DORA / NIS2 / EU AI Act / ZDR across **three separate Responsibilities bullets** and again in the quals block - requirement-tier, no clone inflation, verbatim on all four cited strings.

It still dies on `worth-closing`, for two reasons that are dispositive:

1. **The claim's own premise is partly false.** "Zero Data Retention as a contract construct returns zero hits" is wrong. I verified it directly: `data/curriculum.json` row 71, `subtopics[14]`, **"The LLM-specific compliance surface"** (20 min) - "retention and abuse-monitoring windows and how zero-data-retention agreements change them, the model provider as a named subprocessor with notice obligations" - and row 85, `subtopics[14]`, **"The FDE twist: designing inside the customer's constraints"**, which rehearses the same vocabulary aloud in a mock. The grep searched `Zero Data Retention`; the corpus writes it hyphenated and lowercase.
2. **Frequency is 1/15, and it is a vocabulary gap over machinery that exists.** DORA and NIS2 are scoping-plus-obligation regimes whose transferable parts - incident clocks, subprocessor registers, evidence populations, crosswalks - are row 71 subtopics 3, 4, 7, 8, 10. ISO 42001 is the management-system shape row 71 `subtopics[15]` ("Map once, answer many: crosswalks, questionnaires and machine-readable controls") already teaches via the ISO 27001 / SOC 2 distinction. Closing it costs four acronyms - an evening of reading in the week that one London req is live, not curriculum.

**Classification: vocabulary gap. Landing row would have been 71 (`data/curriculum/70.json`). Displacement required: none, and therefore no eviction is justified** - an eviction is the price of a capability the plan cannot produce, and the plan can produce this.

### Residual that is real but is not a gap

The three lenses surfaced one genuinely uncovered *concept* that no gap claim actually named: **partner / SI / prime orchestration** passed the coverage lens cleanly - ~60 concept probes returned zero hits, including `systems integrator`, `teaming agreement`, `prime contractor`, `flow-down`, `co-sell`, and every Big-Four/GSI firm name. ## 4. WHERE THE THREE TAXONOMIES DISAGREED - AND WHO WAS RIGHT

Four claims were flagged as inter-file contradictions. Only one is a real disagreement; two are metric mismatches dressed as conflicts; one is a denominator artifact. In every case both files were wrong about the plan.

**`knowledge-graphs-graphrag` - 3/95 (ml) vs 1/17 (infrastructure). Infrastructure was right on the count; ML was right on the spread.** The three Scale reqs are seniority clones of one JD - `difflib` similarity 0.9991 / 0.9991 / 0.9990, identical location string. So 3/95 triple-counts one requisition and 1/17 is the honest number. But ML's "one company" is wrong in the other direction: KG-family terms hit 15 distinct JDs across 6 companies on the live boards. Both prescriptions ("a real hole" / "cheap G-54 subtopic") are moot - the gap dies on coverage anyway, at row 58.

**`agent-frameworks-named` - 9/95 (ml) vs 2/17 (infrastructure). ML was right in kind, infrastructure right on risk framing, and both were wrong on the facts.** Of 37 role families containing "agent framework," exactly **one** puts it under Required Qualifications; OpenAI's sits under a literal "Bonus Points" header, Sierra's 24 hits are one *EXAMPLE PROJECTS* bullet describing what the team builds. ML's framing - "a vocabulary gap that costs keyword screens, not interviews" - is the correct grade. Both files then reported 0-1 plan hits against an actual 4 assigned framework resources in row 53, because neither read the `resource` object.

**`managed-cloud-ai-platforms` - 0/95 (ml) vs 5/17 (infrastructure). There is no disagreement; they measured different things.** ML counted *AI-service* names (Bedrock/SageMaker/Vertex/Foundry) = 0. Infrastructure counted *cloud* names (AWS/Azure/GCP) = 5, and separately reported AI services = 0. Both agree the AI-service count is zero. Infrastructure's "field-reality gap, not keyword gap" is the better reading of what matters - but its own supporting quote, restored from truncation, reads "cloud-based machine learning tools and platforms **(e.g. AWS, GCP, Azure)**," which is evidence for row 15, not for a managed-AI-surface gap.

**`voice-speech-agents` - 8/95 (ml) vs "0/17 as a requirement" (infrastructure). Not a contradiction: the infrastructure corpus contains no Sierra and no Decagon by construction (17 reqs across OpenAI/Anthropic/Cohere/Scale).** Both files reach the *same* verdict in their own words - ML files it under "Rare (<10%, Sierra/Decagon-only)," infrastructure under "correctly deprioritised for this target set." The claim manufactured a conflict from two compatible fractions.

**The craft file also double-counted itself:** the EMEA regulatory item is stated twice - once as a "verified partial GAP" in §1 C8 and again as §2 GAP-7 - which inflates the apparent gap count by one before any lens runs.

**The methodological verdict:** all three taxonomies were built by grepping subtopic *names* and `learn` prose. None read `subtopics[].resource`. That single omission produced five of the six false-zero claims in §3B. Any future gap audit must scan `name + learn + resource.label + resource.url`, and must dedup JD bodies by hash before quoting a frequency.

## 6. WHAT I WOULD NOT DO

**I would not manufacture a survivor to justify the exercise.** Eighteen challenged, zero survived, and the discipline that produced the four-item visual-ML decision is the same discipline that produces zero here: the unit of decision is a named subtopic, a named incumbent, and a stated reason the incumbent is worse *for that subtopic*. No gap reached that bar.

**I would not treat `worth-closing`'s 18/18 as proof the lens is a rubber stamp.** It is the strongest lens because it is the only one that asks the question that costs something. Its verdicts are not uniform: five refuted the claim by disproving a factual premise, four by showing the requirement is gated behind a clearance or a company the candidate is not applying to, and nine by showing the capability already exists under other words. A lens that refutes everything is suspicious only if its reasons repeat; these do not.

**I would not add a "partner / SI orchestration" subtopic despite it passing coverage cleanly.** It is the one genuine conceptual hole in the plan. Its only requirement-tier demand - Scale's Deployment Strategist SI-and-prime bullet - sits in the same Requirements block as "Active TS/SCI; Full Scope Polygraph strongly preferred." Building curriculum for a req the candidate cannot be considered for is the definition of a wasted eviction. Log it in the registry as a known, reasoned omission alongside `public-sector-acquisition`; do not schedule an hour against it.

**I would not fix the stale `month` / `hours` keys in the 119 `data/curriculum/NN.json` files as part of this change.** The drift is real and known - `build-curriculum.py` now treats it as a hard error rather than a silent revert - but it is a separate change with a separate risk profile and it is not a precondition for a decision whose curriculum-side output is empty. Coupling a registry text repair to a 119-file reconciliation is exactly the coupling the visual-ML decision refused.

**I would not re-run `renumber-months.py`.** Nothing in this decision touches `Hours` (column 13). Running it would relabel nothing and is contraindicated.

**I would not re-derive gap frequencies from the existing taxonomy files.** All three were built by grepping subtopic names and `learn` bodies without reading `resource`, and by counting postings without deduping location and seniority clones. Any number inherited from them is suspect in both directions - `public-sector-acquisition` was inflated 4×, `delivery-leadership-mentoring` was *conservative*. If frequency is going to drive a scheduling decision, it has to be recounted against deduped bodies with EEO and "Bonus Points" blocks stripped, not carried forward.

**I would not delete `emea-ai-regulation` from the registry along with the other false-premise entries.** It is the only claim in the set whose job-market evidence survived intact at requirement tier with no clone inflation, at £190k-£230k. It should stay on the board as a watch item with a corrected `whyNotCovered` - the right response to one live London req is four acronyms read the week it appears, not a curriculum row.
