**A. The case for zero**

**1. Source reliability is measurable and bad.** 14 of 28 entries had `claimAccurate: false` — ML Visualized (advertised diffusion models; its own search index returns zero hits for "diffusion", "transformer", "attention"), Distill.pub (advertised AutoML across 45 articles containing none), Gradient Descent Visualizer (advertised SGD/momentum/Adam; grep returns zero hits for all three), REINFORCEjs (advertised policy gradients; the spec exposes exactly `qlearn` and `sarsa`), Embedding Projector (advertised GloVe; zero occurrences in the 1.86 MB bundle). 25 of 26 non-dropped entries had their row mapping refuted; only K-Means (Naftali Harris) survived intact. Two entries duplicate other entries — "TF Playground spiral preset" is the same app at the same origin as "TensorFlow Playground" (one URL fragment), and "Image Kernels Explained" is a page inside the separately-listed Setosa. Two are dead. "28 sites" is 24 artifacts. Someone wrong about half of what is checkable earns no credit on what isn't checkable — pedagogical value.

**2. Day-zero rot is ~20%; the mapped rows are 18–23 months out.** Broken at verification: Gradient Descent Visualizer (cert expired 2026-08-28, full interstitial), AttentionViz (HTTPS page hard-coded to `http://18.219.70.154:8500`, blocked permanently by its own HSTS header), both R2D3 essays at the listed `www.` host (Cloudflare 522), REINFORCEjs (MathJax over `http://` blocked — every equation renders as raw LaTeX), Setosa's Markov playground. Of ~64 non-trivial mappings, 46 (72%) land in months 18–23; rows 111–119 are months 22–23. You would shelve AngularJS 1.x, d3 v3, an archived read-only Polymer 1.x app, and a TensorSpace toolchain whose only build path is `ubuntu:16.04` plus a deleted PPA — against sessions in 2028.

**3. Plan criteria are production-mode; the sites are recognition-mode, by their own admission.** Row 111 wants a backward pass matching finite differences to 1e-7; row 112 wants Adam from scratch matching `torch.optim` to 1e-6; row 113 wants `gradcheck` passing a custom `autograd.Function`; row 116 wants 1/√d_k derived on paper. The verified notes concede it: TF Playground "you finish with feel and no ability to compute"; Setosa "you will not leave able to compute an SVD, derive the OLS normal equations, or write a convolution"; Embedding Projector "unable to implement t-SNE or UMAP"; R2D3 part 1 "a feeling rather than a skill"; DBSCAN "unable to tune it on a real dataset." Diffusion Explainer is net-negative — zero occurrences of "attention", "VAE", "latent", "sampler" in its JavaScript, so it teaches a vocabulary you must unlearn to read `diffusers`.

**4. Five install vivid wrong pictures ahead of the rows that refute them.** TF Playground's only loss is squared error used for classification — row 99 (month 11) requires deriving cross-entropy from MLE; its seven hand-picked features are "the opposite of the representation learning that matters at scale." R2D3 part 2 sells the U-curve as universal, four months before rows 111–116 where overparameterised nets descend again past interpolation. ConvNet Playground teaches pre-CLIP late-layer retrieval; row 118 wants symmetric InfoNCE from memory. GAN Lab loads with "Use pre-trained model" checked, so you watch equilibrium and believe you watched training.

**5. It misses the interview surface entirely.** Rows 50–66 are 17 topics and 225 hours: prompt engineering as code (51), tool use and MCP (52), agents (53), RAG at depth (54), guardrails and injection (56), permission-aware retrieval (58), evals as infrastructure (59 — 18h, the largest topic in the file), observability (60), gateways and fallbacks (61), drift and registries (63), cost engineering (65). The list touches two rows in that span: 50, and 62 weakly via Netron. Fifteen of seventeen production topics get nothing.

**6. The mapping is a keyword pile-up.** Row 116 (17h, whose job is that you *write* a transformer) accumulates bbycroft 40 + Transformer Explainer 30 + Illustrated Transformer 35 + Illustrated GPT-2 40 + BertViz 25 + Netron 15 = 185 minutes of spectating, 18% of the row, on top of Karpathy's build and The Annotated Transformer. Row 114 gets five competing primers; row 111 gets three.

**7. Two recommendations are destructive.** bbycroft and Transformer Explainer are both proposed as `replace-do` on row 50, whose current `do` is Karpathy's "Let's build GPT." bbycroft has no adjustable parameter anywhere and a `{A,B,C}` vocabulary; Transformer Explainer caps prompts at 12 words behind a 626 MB download. Row 50's depth is hallucination mechanisms and model-card reading; its first two subtopics are BPE merges and token accounting. Neither covers tokenization, RLHF, scaling laws, or model cards.

**8. True cost is ~25 hours, not 12.5.** 748 advertised minutes plus 20–30 min per entry for row assignment, caveat capture (TF Playground alone needs three), and mandatory re-verification at use time. That is 1.5 topic rows. On a 23-month plan the dominant failure mode is attrition, and every optional item is attrition surface.

**B. What survives — four, 90 minutes**

The argument above kills anything whose value is intuition the plan already bought (3Blue1Brown 94/95; StatQuest 96/97/101/102/107; CS231n 114; CS224N 115; Karpathy 111/112/113/116). It does not kill a production tool, a live checker for a stated mastery criterion, or a falsifiable experiment the existing `do` doesn't stage.

**Netron → row 113, 15 min.** Not a demo — a maintained, installable tool (`pip install netron`, brew) whose value rises with production work: reading tensor shapes and op attributes off a customer's ONNX or quantised export. No client-side rot exposure; the desktop build survives netron.app. Record the trap: a plain `.pth` state_dict renders as a flat weight inventory, not a graph. Footnote on row 62.

**CNN Explainer → row 114, 30 min.** Row 114's first depth clause is hand-computing output shape, parameter count and receptive field; the Input Size / Padding / Kernel Size / Stride widget is literally a checker for it, and the Interactive Formula View shows real kernel arithmetic. Peer-reviewed, works today, convolution arithmetic is invariant. Note: 10 fixed classes, 64×64 uploads, frozen weights, repo dead since Oct 2023.

**Transformer Explainer → row 50, 30 min, subtopic slot only — never `replace-do`.** Row 50 explicitly requires "temperature, top_p, top_k, stop, seed, logprobs," which Karpathy's talk does not show visually. This is the one entry where you turn those knobs on real GPT-2 124M weights, which defeats the toy-problem objection that sinks TF Playground and GAN Lab. Carry: 626 MB cold load, 12-word cap, desktop only, 2019 architecture.

**K-Means (Naftali Harris) → row 107, 15 min.** The only mapping in 28 not refuted, and Lloyd's algorithm is unchanged since 1957, so staleness has no purchase. Fills a hole ISLP Ch12 leaves: initialisation sensitivity. One five-minute experiment — "Randomly" init three times until a bad local optimum appears, then read the k-means++ prose.

**C. Notable kills**

**bbycroft's LLM Visualization** is the strongest rejection and fails on redundancy, not quality — maintained (bugfix 2026-08-10), precise, excellent, but 40 minutes of inference-only viewing adds nothing to "derive 1/√d_k" once you have Karpathy's build and The Annotated Transformer. Hold it as a 10-minute debugging aid if the causal-mask or head-splitting failure row 116 names actually bites. **Seeing Theory** is the closest miss (Beta-Binomial widget vs row 96's posterior-by-hand), beaten by Blitzstein L2–6 plus BMH Ch4. **Distill.pub** is a 20-hour archive of InceptionV1-era interpretability — cite one article on demand, don't shelve it. Cut the rest. **ML Visualiser** should never be reconsidered: a CNN that reads the ground-truth label and reports 70–90% confidence is not a weak resource, it is a lie with a UI.

Net: 24 real artifacts in, 4 out, 90 minutes against 1,588 hours (0.09%), each with an owner row, a falsifiable exercise, and a written caveat.
