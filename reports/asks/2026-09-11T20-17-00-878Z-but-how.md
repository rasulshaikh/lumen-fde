## Answer

Your written answer is one sentence, so I will score it against the three acceptance checks first, then show what a passing answer looks like, then give one next step.

The acceptance checks for this review question are:

1. A rejected lead has a reason and a route back to marketing.
2. You can explain why a contact lifecycle is different from a deal stage.
3. Every metric has a denominator and a time window.

Your sentence covers check 1 in spirit (you do say there has to be a reason for rejection and that the feedback has to reach marketing), but it does not name the reasons, does not name who owns the feedback loop, and does not commit to a specific taxonomy such as wrong size, no budget, no timing, duplicate, or spam. So check 1 is half met.

Your sentence does not cover check 2 at all. You treat "lead" and "deal" as the same object throughout. The module's whole point is that one contact can sit in a lifecycle stage (subscriber, lead, MQL, SQL, customer) while one deal between that contact and your company moves through its own stages (qualified, proposal, closed-won) on a different clock. A "bad lead" complaint is almost always a complaint about lifecycle-to-deal hand-off, and you cannot debug that without separating the two clocks. Check 2 is not met.

Your sentence gestures at check 3 by saying you would look at acceptance rate and conversion rate, but it does not state denominators or a time window. Without those, those words are slogans. Lead-to-SQL is leads sent. SQL-to-close is SQLs created. Window is the last 90 days, not "recently". Check 3 is not met.

Two of three checks missed, so this is a re-answer rather than a pass.

What a passing answer sounds like

In plain language, written so a sales lead and a marketing lead would both nod.

"I would not buy anything yet. I would pull the last 90 days of leads and split them into four counts: sent, accepted by sales as SQL, rejected with a reason, and closed-won regardless of acceptance. The rejection reason is chosen from a small fixed list: wrong size, no budget, no timing, duplicate, spam. Lead-to-SQL is computed as SQLs divided by leads sent, so the denominator matches. SQL-to-close is SQLs divided by closed-won, again matching denominators. Sales disputes a lead by selecting a reason and clicking reject, and the CRM writes that reason back to a feedback queue that marketing owns, so the loop closes in the system, not in a meeting. A deal stage is where one opportunity between us and the contact sits, like qualified or proposal or closed-won. A lifecycle stage is where the contact sits, like subscriber, lead, MQL, SQL, or customer. A contact can be in lifecycle stage SQL without a deal existing yet, and a contact can have a deal in proposal while their lifecycle stage stays as customer. The sales complaint usually confuses those clocks, and separating them is what tells us whether marketing is sending the wrong people, sales is mishandling the right people, or both."

That answer hits all three checks: reason plus route back, lifecycle versus deal explained in one sentence each, denominators and window stated.
