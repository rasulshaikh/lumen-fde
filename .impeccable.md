# Impeccable — Lumen

## Design Context

### Users
Rasul, alone, using this as a personal command centre while preparing over 13 months
(916 hours, 119 topics) for senior Forward-Deployed Engineer roles. Two modes of use:
a weekly review where a very large plan has to become legible in ten minutes, and a
daily session where he opens one topic and works through its syllabus. Not a team tool,
not a product with users to onboard — a desk he returns to.

### Brand Personality
Focused, candid, quietly ambitious. It should feel like a well-made field notebook
rather than an analytics product: warm, worked-in, honest about the size of the task.
It never flatters. When 916 hours is 13 months, it says so.

### Aesthetic Direction
**Ember Studio**, adapted. Terracotta warmth over stone neutrals, a serif/sans pairing
carrying the craft, amber reserved for genuine attention. Light theme — this is used in
daylight at a desk, and dark mode here would be costume.

Two deliberate departures from the Ember file, both recorded so they are not mistaken
for drift:

1. **No coloured side stripes.** Ember specifies 4px left stripes on project cards, a
   terracotta left border on selected cards and a 3px left accent bar on nav items.
   That pattern is the most recognisable AI-dashboard tell. Ember's *intent* — terracotta
   marks the active thing — is honoured with full borders, background tints and weight.
2. **Petrona replaces Playfair Display.** Playfair is a training-data default. Petrona
   is a warm variable serif with more character at heading sizes and far less ubiquity.
   Source Sans 3 and Fira Code are kept exactly as Ember specifies.

**Anti-references** (from PRODUCT.md, still binding): no generic SaaS analytics wall,
no decorative charts that carry no decision, no rainbow status colours, no motivational
copy that hides the real workload.

### Design Principles
1. **Pace before volume.** How far behind or ahead he is matters more than totals.
2. **The next action sits next to its evidence.** Never make him navigate to act.
3. **Colour encodes state, never decoration.** Terracotta means active or interactive.
   Amber means attention. Everything else is stone.
4. **Density is a feature.** 119 rows and 2,236 syllabus parts must stay scannable;
   whitespace is bought where it aids scanning, not sprinkled evenly.
5. **WCAG AA is a hard floor.** Six of Ember's raw values fail as text on this canvas,
   so text tokens are derived at the lightest value that still clears 4.5:1 (3:1 for
   control boundaries). Opacity is never used to mute — muting is an explicit colour.

### The mark
A bracketed serif **L** reversed out of a terracotta field — `app/icon.svg`, the single
source of truth that `scripts/build-icons.mjs` derives the favicon, Apple icon and link
preview from.

It came out of a six-concept judge panel that rejected all six candidates (0/3 ship
votes each). Its three findings are the standing rules for any future mark here:

1. **One colour.** Every rejected candidate used terracotta as a *sub-part* and it read
   as a progress fill, disappeared under monochrome, or sat at 2.94:1 against ink. The
   silhouette must carry 100% of the identity, so the mark survives pinned tabs,
   forced-colors and dark backgrounds.
2. **Do not depict a nameable object.** Every candidate that did collided with a stock
   icon set — Tabler `notebook`, Lucide `tally-5`, the VLC cone, a pause button, a
   scrollbar thumb. A letterform collides with nothing.
3. **Meaning that needs an essay is not meaning.** The craft lives in the drawing — the
   brackets flowing out of the stem — not in a concept that has to be explained.

Every coordinate is even, so at 16px (2 units = 1 device pixel) each letter edge lands
on a whole pixel. Centring it optically costs that alignment and softens every edge
(23 antialiased pixels → 36), so it deliberately sits one unit left of true centre.
Do not "fix" that. Verify any change by rendering at 16/20/34/220, not by eye at 512.

### Accessibility contract
AA contrast on all text, 3:1 on control boundaries, visible focus on every interactive
element, status never conveyed by colour alone, reduced-motion respected, and touch
targets at or above 24px (44px on coarse pointers).

Two things this contract deliberately does **not** demand, so a later pass does not
"fix" them into something worse:

- **Card hairlines stay at 1.4:1.** WCAG 1.4.11 governs boundaries *required to identify
  a control*. `.panel` and `.metric` are containers, not controls — they are identified
  by their content and surface. Pushing every card edge to 3:1 turns 119 rows into a
  hard grid and buries the borders that do carry meaning. `--line-strong` (3:1+) is the
  token for anything a user can actually operate; `--line` is for decoration.
- **Colour tokens are derived against the peach `--primary-soft` tint, not just the
  canvas.** The "Current focus" card and expanded syllabus panels sit on that tint, and
  it is *lighter* than the surface, so tokens that pass on cream can still fail there.
  Every text token clears 4.5:1 on all three of `--bg`, `--surface` and `--primary-soft`.

Verified in-browser rather than by arithmetic: 189 rendered text/background combinations
and 12 control boundaries across all 8 tabs, zero failures. Measure via canvas pixel
readback — Chrome serialises computed `oklch()` colours as `oklch()`/`lab()`, so naive
`rgb()` parsing silently reads the lightness as a red channel and reports everything
as failing.
