# Impeccable — Lumen

## Design Context

### Users
Rasul, alone, using this as a personal command centre while preparing over 13 months
(916 hours, 119 topics) for senior Forward-Deployed Engineer roles. Two modes of use:
a weekly review where a very large plan has to become legible in ten minutes, and a
daily session where he opens one topic and works through its syllabus. Not a team tool,
not a product with users to onboard — a desk he returns to.

### Brand Personality
Focused, candid, quietly ambitious. It should feel like a well-made field notebook
rather than an analytics product: warm, worked-in, honest about the size of the task.
It never flatters. When 916 hours is 13 months, it says so.

### Aesthetic Direction
**Sorbet Tonal** (superseded Ember Studio, Sept 2026, at Rasul's request). A four-blob
pastel gradient — lavender, peach, seafoam, cream, every stop held near 25% chroma —
runs across the canvas; pure paper-white cards sit on top. The contrast between the soft
gradient page and the bright white cards is the entire design. One family, Manrope, with
JetBrains Mono for eyebrows, metadata and table numerals. Mauve is the only chromatic
accent. Light theme.

Four recorded departures from the Sorbet spec sheet, so they are not mistaken for drift:

1. **Three colours darkened for AA.** The spec grades its own accent-on-canvas **FAIL**
   (2.74:1) and suggests `#7c5d9c`, but that was measured against flat `#F4EFE9`. The
   gradient's darkest composited pixel is `rgb(217,205,227)` — the lavender blob — and
   `#7c5d9c` still fails there, as does the spec's `#6B6973` graphite for body text.
   Both were darkened until they clear 4.5:1 against that worst-case pixel, and
   `--line-strong` was made opaque so control boundaries reach 3:1. `#A088B8` survives
   as `--accent` for decorative fills only, never text.
2. **Compact density.** The spec's default is comfortable, but its own Density table
   sanctions compact "for information-dense — tables, dashboards", which is what 119
   rows and 2,236 syllabus parts are. Section gaps are dashboard-scale, not the
   editorial 128px.
3. **Disabled states muted by colour, not `opacity: 0.4`.** Opacity dims the label with
   the box and stacks with whatever sits behind it. Nothing in this app uses fractional
   opacity.
4. **A dark theme, which Sorbet does not ship.** Derived, not quoted: the same four
   blobs at a tenth of the alpha over deep ink, cards lifted off the canvas instead of
   sunk into it. Follows the OS until the user picks a side, then persists.
5. **A three-stop mauve ramp doing the grading.** The flat read came from one accent
   doing every job. Sorbet already ships `#A088B8 / #C7B4D8 / #E0D2EA` as a chart
   palette; used as a real ramp it gives depth without a second hue, and elevation is
   graded too (a metric tile must not carry a modal's shadow).
6. **Two functional status colours beyond mauve.** The spec allows no second chromatic
   accent, but success and danger are load-bearing here (topic status, comp-reality
   probability). They are held to desaturated tones inside the sorbet family and are
   never the only signal — status always carries a label too.

**Anti-references** (from PRODUCT.md, still binding): no generic SaaS analytics wall,
no decorative charts that carry no decision, no rainbow status colours, no motivational
copy that hides the real workload.

### Design Principles
1. **Pace before volume.** How far behind or ahead he is matters more than totals.
2. **The next action sits next to its evidence.** Never make him navigate to act.
3. **Colour encodes state, never decoration.** Mauve means active or interactive.
   Everything else is ink or graphite; the gradient is ambience, never information.
4. **Density is a feature.** 119 rows and 2,236 syllabus parts must stay scannable;
   whitespace is bought where it aids scanning, not sprinkled evenly.
5. **WCAG AA is a hard floor**, and on a gradient canvas that means measuring against
   the *darkest* pixel the gradient produces, not the flat base colour. Text tokens are
   derived at the lightest value that still clears 4.5:1 there (3:1 for control
   boundaries). Opacity is never used to mute — muting is an explicit colour.

### The mark
A bracketed serif **L** reversed out of an ink field (terracotta under Ember) — `app/icon.svg`, the single
source of truth that `scripts/build-icons.mjs` derives the favicon, Apple icon and link
preview from.

It came out of a six-concept judge panel that rejected all six candidates (0/3 ship
votes each). Its three findings are the standing rules for any future mark here:

1. **One colour.** Every rejected candidate used terracotta as a *sub-part* and it read
   as a progress fill, disappeared under monochrome, or sat at 2.94:1 against ink. The
   silhouette must carry 100% of the identity, so the mark survives pinned tabs,
   forced-colors and dark backgrounds.
2. **Do not depict a nameable object.** Every candidate that did collided with a stock
   icon set — Tabler `notebook`, Lucide `tally-5`, the VLC cone, a pause button, a
   scrollbar thumb. A letterform collides with nothing.
3. **Meaning that needs an essay is not meaning.** The craft lives in the drawing — the
   brackets flowing out of the stem — not in a concept that has to be explained.

Every coordinate is even, so at 16px (2 units = 1 device pixel) each letter edge lands
on a whole pixel. Centring it optically costs that alignment and softens every edge
(23 antialiased pixels → 36), so it deliberately sits one unit left of true centre.
Do not "fix" that. Verify any change by rendering at 16/20/34/220, not by eye at 512.

### Responsive tiers
Verified by measurement across eleven viewports in both orientations (360x640 up to
2560x1440): zero horizontal page scroll, zero clipped text, zero interactive targets
under 24px, at every one.

Ask Lumen has exactly **three** size tiers, and they live in one consolidated block at
the end of globals.css. Eight competing `.ask-panel` rules had accumulated across seven
breakpoints, and the bug they hid was the rail breakpoint sitting at 1201px: a 1024x768
tablet or a 1180x820 iPad Pro got a 91-94% fullscreen takeover despite having room for a
rail beside the plan.

| tier | width | panel covers | dashboard still visible |
|---|---|---|---|
| >= 1000px — docked rail, shell yields a gutter | clamp(360px, 30vw, 440px) | 17-33% | 576-1440px |
| 700-999px — overlay rail, shell does not move | min(380px, 52vw) | 38-48% | behind the panel |
| < 700px — bottom sheet | inset 8px, top `max(72px, 14svh)` | 81-82% | a 52-130px strip |

Every tier keeps context visible, phones included: the sheet stops short of the top edge
so the topbar and the current section stay on screen, which says *what* you are asking
about and gives the sheet somewhere to be dismissed to instead of reading as a new
screen. The strip is proportional (`14svh`) with a floor, so tall phones give up more of
it than short ones. There is no scrim — the strip is live context, not decoration.

A separate `max-height: 460px` rule handles landscape phones, where the panel is only
~300px tall: the intro copy is dropped and the strip shrinks to `max(52px, 10svh)` so
the prompts and the input still fit.

### The assistant's mark
Ask Lumen carries a **six-armed printer's asterisk** in mauve, not the L. Two reasons it
is a separate mark: a product should not show its logo twice on one screen, and the
thing it replaced — a four-point ✦ — is the single most recognisable generative-AI tell
in current UI. The asterisk belongs to the type family the brand is built on, and three
crossed strokes survive 14px where anything with interior detail turns to mud. It is
`AskMark` in app/brand.tsx, used in the trigger, the FAB, the panel header and the empty
state. The favicon stays the L.

### Surface separation — the check that was missing
Text contrast is not sufficient. A card can pass every AA test on its text and still be
invisible, because AA says nothing about whether a **surface** is distinguishable from
the surface behind it. The first dark pass had canvas→card at **1.10:1** and card→chip
at **1.15:1** — every text ratio passed, and the UI still read as an undifferentiated
black field. Audit these three every time, in both themes:

| step | floor | why |
|---|---|---|
| canvas → card | 1.25:1 against the *worst* gradient pixel | the card must read where the colour is |
| card → chip | 1.20:1 | nested surfaces must not merge |
| ink vs muted on card | ratio ≥ 1.7 | otherwise text tones collapse into one grey |

On a gradient canvas these fight each other, and that tension sets the ceiling on how
strong the gradient can be: push the blobs harder and the brightest blob region reaches
card luminance, so cards vanish exactly where the colour is; lift the text to compensate
and ink / ink-2 / muted converge. Dark blob alpha is 0.135 because that is the strongest
value that still satisfies all three rows above. Do not raise it without re-solving.

### Accessibility contract
AA contrast on all text, 3:1 on control boundaries, visible focus on every interactive
element, status never conveyed by colour alone, reduced-motion respected, and touch
targets at or above 24px (44px on coarse pointers).

Prose measure (66ch) is for running prose only. Applied to every `<p>` it turns
dashboard panels into narrow ribbons with dead space beside them; where a lead paragraph
sits in a wide card, pair it with something (the syllabus lead sits beside the three
entry-point resources) rather than widening the measure.

Two things this contract deliberately does **not** demand, so a later pass does not
"fix" them into something worse:

- **Card hairlines stay at 1.4:1.** WCAG 1.4.11 governs boundaries *required to identify
  a control*. `.panel` and `.metric` are containers, not controls — they are identified
  by their content and surface. Pushing every card edge to 3:1 turns 119 rows into a
  hard grid and buries the borders that do carry meaning. `--line-strong` (3:1+) is the
  token for anything a user can actually operate; `--line` is for decoration.
- **Colour tokens are derived against the worst background a token can land on**, not
  the nominal one. Under Ember that was the peach `--primary-soft` tint; under Sorbet it
  is the darkest gradient pixel, `rgb(217,205,227)`. Translucent tints sitting directly
  on the canvas are the trap — that is why `.hero-note` is paper white, which is what the
  Sorbet spec demands of cards anyway.

Verified in-browser rather than by arithmetic, per theme: 221 text/background
combinations and 171 control boundaries across all 8 tabs in **each** of light and dark,
zero failures. Each theme is judged against its own worst-case gradient pixel — the
*darkest* in light (`rgb(218,206,225)`), the *lightest* in dark (`rgb(46,47,51)`),
since that is the binding constraint when type inverts.

Measuring in a hidden browser pane needs one guard: the pane does not composite frames,
so a CSS transition started there stays pinned at its start value forever and reads as a
catastrophic failure (a quiz option measured 1.13:1 that was in fact fine). Hold
transitions off for the whole audit. Measure via canvas pixel readback — Chrome serialises computed colours
as `oklch()`/`lab()`, so naive `rgb()` parsing silently reads the lightness as a red
channel and reports everything as failing. To find the worst canvas pixel, repaint the
CSS gradient stack onto a `<canvas>` and scan it; the pseudo-element carrying it is not
reachable from `getComputedStyle`.
